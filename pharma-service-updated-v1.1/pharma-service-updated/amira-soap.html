The Chemistry

Why Tallow Feels Different on Your Skin

Tallow's fatty acid profile is remarkably close to human skin lipids. That's not marketing — it's biochemistry. Here's what's inside the 58%.

~40%

Oleic Acid

Emollient

Skin-softening. Penetrates the outer layer and restores moisture from within. Same acid dominant in olive oil.

~26%

Palmitic Acid

Structure

Creates the bar's firm texture and stable, creamy lather. Also a major component of human sebum.

~20%

Stearic Acid

Barrier

Leaves a light lipid film on the skin after washing. This is why tallow soaps don't feel "stripped." Your barrier stays intact.

~4%

Myristic Acid

Cleansing

Provides the balanced cleansing action without over-stripping. Works with the other fatty acids to create a soap that cleans effectively without destroying the barrier.

Why this matters: These four fatty acids together mirror the lipid composition of human skin more closely than any vegetable oil. Modern palm and coconut soaps strip the skin — that "squeaky clean" feeling is your barrier being damaged. A 58% tallow bar leaves a fine lipid film that preserves barrier function. This is centuries-old soap science that the industry abandoned in the 1970s because palm oil was cheaper.
